# 01 — Compliance & Billing

**Kingdom Financial Services — presented by Noel Duran**

The legal foundation for how KFS charges for credit repair. Everything in this division — pricing, intake, scripts, letters — is built to stay inside these rules.

---

## The Core Rule: $0 Up Front, Ever

Under the federal Credit Repair Organizations Act (CROA), **15 U.S.C. §1679b(b)**, a credit repair organization may not charge or receive *any* money for a service before that service has been **fully performed**.

- Calling it a "setup fee," "audit fee," "consultation fee," or "first month" does not help — the ban follows the substance, not the label.
- This is the #1 enforcement trigger in the industry. KFS collects nothing — no deposit, no retainer, no card on file charged early — before the work it pays for is done and documented.

## The Billing Model: Per-Verified-Deletion Milestones

KFS charges on the per-deletion fee schedule in `02-price-list.md`. Each line item is a **milestone**:

1. The service for that line is the removal (or correction) of one specific item **on one specific bureau**.
2. The fee is **earned only when the deletion is verified** on that bureau's updated report.
3. KFS then issues an **itemized invoice** for milestones actually achieved, with the before/after reports in the client file as proof of performance.
4. No milestone, no charge. If an item is verified as accurate and stays, that line is not billed.

This structure is the classic CROA-safe model: every dollar charged ties to a verified, documented result, and payment always follows performance.

### Alternative: monthly in arrears

For ongoing work (monitoring, successive rounds), billing monthly **in arrears** — for a period already worked — is also CROA-legal, because the services billed have already been performed. Market reference: national firms charge $50–$150/month (Credit Firm $49.99; Sky Blue $79; Credit Saint $79.99–$139.99; The Credit People $99). If a monthly arrangement is ever used, the first month is billed only *after* the first dispute round is actually filed — never at the review call, never as a "setup fee."

## The Trap: Telemarketing Sales Rule — 16 CFR §310.4(a)(2)

If credit repair is **sold by telemarketing** (phone or video sales conversations, including inbound calls responding to ads), payment cannot even be **requested** until **both**:

- the entire timeframe represented for the services has expired, **and**
- the client has been given a consumer report, issued **more than six months after** the promised results were achieved, demonstrating those results.

That structure kills cash flow, so legitimate operators avoid selling through telemarketing. KFS practice:

- **Sign clients face-to-face** (in-person meeting) — the classic TSR exemption route.
- Text/chat intake (e.g., WhatsApp messaging) is fine for scheduling and explanation; treat voice/video **sales** calls as risky.
- Get an NJ attorney to confirm the exact sales-flow structure before scaling.

## The Contract Packet (signed before any paid work)

| Document | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Written service contract | CROA-compliant: services described, cost basis, timeline estimate, guarantees (none) stated accurately |
| "Consumer Credit File Rights Under State and Federal Law" disclosure | 15 U.S.C. §1679c — must be given to the client **before** signing |
| Notice of Cancellation | 15 U.S.C. §1679e — client may cancel within **3 business days** of signing, no penalty |
| Written authorization | Client authorizes KFS to obtain their credit reports and dispute on their behalf |

No contract packet, no case. The free review happens before the packet and is always free.

## Prohibited Claims & Conduct (CROA §1679b(a))

- ❌ Promising to remove **accurate, current** negative information.
- ❌ Guaranteeing a score increase, a deletion, or a timeline for results.
- ❌ Advising a client to dispute accurate debts as "not mine."
- ❌ Advising identity tricks — a new EIN used as an SSN, a CPN, or any "file segregation" scheme (that is fraud).
- ❌ Asking a client to waive any CROA right — waivers are void.
- Violations expose KFS to actual damages (or the full amount the client paid, whichever is greater), punitive damages, and attorney's fees — plus treble-damage risk under the NJ Consumer Fraud Act.

**What KFS disputes:** only items that are **inaccurate, unverifiable, incomplete, or obsolete**, each with an item-specific factual basis stated in the letter.

## Record-Keeping (every charge must be provable)

- **Before/after bureau reports** saved in the client file for every billed deletion — the invoice must trace to a verified report change.
- **Case log** per client: bureau, item, dispute reason, date mailed, response deadline (~30 days), outcome.
- **Copies of every letter** sent on the client's behalf, given to the client.
- Client files live in the client's own secured folder — **never in this repo** (see `README.md` data rules).
